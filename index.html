AI on your data

The worries are reasonable. Here's what we do about each one.

We ran an AI analyst on real payments data for months and logged every time it was wrong. These answers come from that log, not from a vendor deck.

"It will make up numbers."

It rarely gets the SQL wrong. It does misread what the SQL returns: in our logs, 1 in 6 messages was a correction. So every headline number is reconciled against a second source, and every claim links back to the query that produced it.

"Our data isn't clean enough for AI."

Often true, and fixable. Freshness checks, row-count reconciliation and metric definitions in code come first. Then the agent works on numbers that already agree with each other.

"Who's accountable when it's wrong?"

A named person. The agent does the legwork and drafts the write-up. It never publishes. Anything that reaches a customer, a board or the press is signed off by someone who knows roughly what the answer should be.

"Governance, privacy, the EU AI Act."

Agents get read-only roles, and credentials stay out of prompts. Personal data is masked or aggregated before an agent sees it, and every query is logged. You get an audit trail you can actually hand to someone.

"People already paste data into chatbots."

Then give them a sanctioned route that beats the shortcut: an agent on the real warehouse with your definitions built in, instead of a spreadsheet export in a personal account.

"Chat-with-your-data never survives real questions."

Agreed. So we start with your hardest recurring question and track how often answers need correcting. We widen the scope only once that number is low.

Projects along the way

Payments · Diagnostics

The drop that wasn't

Headline approval fell, and a provider was about to take the blame.

Split into mix and rate, every segment had held steady or improved. A surge of first-time customers, who approve less often everywhere, had diluted the blend.

→ A costly re-route was avoided. The effort went into new-customer onboarding instead.

Orchestration · Monitoring

Outages caught in minutes, not mornings

Provider incidents were surfacing through customer complaints, hours late.

We built near-live views by provider, payment method and rail, with alerting that tells a real incident apart from normal overnight wobble.

→ Incidents are flagged as they happen, and failover's real recovery rate is measured rather than assumed.

Platform · Data engineering

From one giant table to a schema people can read

A single monolithic table, slow refreshes, and a new region to launch.

We moved to zero-ETL replication, ran Airflow on Kubernetes, normalised raw JSON events into tables that mirror the product, and documented it all in dbt.

→ Fresher data, faster queries, and a US region live on the same patterns.

Fintech · Strategy

Where tokenisation actually pays

"Should we roll out network tokens, and to whom?"

We matched customers like for like and controlled for confounds. The uplift sits in repeat traffic, and creating the token too close to the first payment can quietly erase it.

→ A rollout plan with evidence a partner could check, presented in QBRs.

Method

The checklist every answer goes through.

Most wrong answers in analytics, human or AI, come from a skipped check, not from bad maths. These seven steps run on every engagement, and our agents run them too.

  1. Is the data whole?Freshness, partial days, time zones and silent load failures are checked first.
  2. Segment before concludingBy date, product, method, cohort, and new vs returning.
  3. Hunt the confoundsTagging changes, broken lookups and definitions that drift mid-window.
  4. Split mix from rateDid performance change, or did the traffic?
  5. Walk the funnelWhich attempt, which provider and which response code?
  6. Benchmark against peersIs it you, or the whole market?
  7. Lead with the answerThe "so what" first, then the evidence, then the caveats.
